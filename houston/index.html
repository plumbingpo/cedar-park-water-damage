What Usually Goes Wrong in Houston Homes

Water damage does not always arrive as a sudden gush across the kitchen. Here is what homeowners around Houston phone us about most, along with what usually sits behind each one.

Hidden leaks: A pinhole in a supply line or a loose drain fitting can drip inside a wall for months. Clues include flooring that feels spongy, blistered paint and a higher water bill you cannot explain. Thermal imaging and moisture readings let us map how far it spread before anything gets cut open. Even a slow leak matters, because wood and drywall that stay damp will rot and grow mold.

Sewage: A blocked sewer line sends waste back into the house, usually at the lowest shower, toilet or floor drain. It is typically caused by roots in the line, built-up grease or a municipal sewer overwhelmed during a downpour. Sewage counts as Category 3, the most contaminated class, and it is not safe to clean up bare-handed. Carpet, padding and other absorbent materials that got wet usually cannot be saved.

Storms: During a heavy storm, runoff may collect along the foundation faster than drains can carry it off. From there it comes in under doors, through window wells and across garage floors. Because surface water carries dirt and chemicals from the ground it passed over, it is handled as unsanitary. Homes across Houston can see this in any season with a strong enough storm.

Crawlspace: In homes built over a crawl space, few people look underneath, so moisture there builds up unseen. Humidity stays high down there when a pipe drips, the ground cover is damaged or water pools after rain. Over time that leads to sagging insulation, musty odors indoors and soft or cupped flooring. Our team removes the water, dries the framing and can point out what is letting moisture in.

Condensate: Cooling equipment produces a steady stream of condensation whenever it runs in damp weather. It is meant to exit through a narrow drain tube, and that tube plugs up with slime and debris. When it backs up, the pan overflows into the ceiling, closet or hallway around the air handler. In Houston we often find this only after a stain or sagging drywall gives it away, so a yearly drain check helps.

Found Water in Your Houston Home? Start Here

What you do before help reaches your Houston address has a real effect on how the job goes. Safety comes before saving anything. If water is near outlets, cords or the electrical panel, stay out of it, and switch off power to that area only if you can reach the breaker without stepping in water. If the water is from a sewer or toilet backup, do not touch it at all.

Then shut the water off. Close the valve at the fixture if there is one, or the main shutoff for the house. After that, carry light items such as rugs, documents, electronics and chairs somewhere dry. Slide plastic or aluminum foil beneath the feet of anything too heavy to move, which stops stains and swelling.

Take photos and video of every affected room before you clean up, because your insurer will want to see the damage as it was. Mop or towel up small amounts of clean water if it is safe, and leave the rest to the crew heading to Houston.
